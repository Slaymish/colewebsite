Cole Anderson — portfolio — three routes

Three ways to build the same work.

Same twelve projects, same two disciplines, three different arguments about who Cole is. These are low fidelity on purpose — the pictures are tone blocks, so what you are judging is structure, type and pace, not photography. Every route below is rendered live, not screenshotted, and two of the three are interactive.

Route A

Call Sheet

A production index rather than a gallery. The work reads as a body of credits before it reads as pictures — title, format, year, set in columns, with one still on screen at a time in a frame that swaps as you move down the list.

What makes it stick

One image on screen at a time, and it changes as you read. That is the whole idea, and it is the opposite of the thumbnail grid every other portfolio opens with. The amber tick is the only colour in the design and it appears on exactly one row at a time.

States

Empty index keeps its rules and column heads with a single row reading “Nothing published yet”. The preview frame holds its 3:2 and shows the tone block until the still decodes, so nothing jumps.

The catch

Hover does not exist on touch, so mobile is a genuinely different layout rather than a squeezed one — square thumbnail left, title and year right. Two layouts to build and maintain.

Route B

Plate

An exhibition monograph. Paper, a quarter of the width given to a margin that holds almost nothing, and one plate at a time. Captions sit in the margin beside their plate the way they do in a printed book, never underneath it.

What makes it stick

The margin behaves like the gutter of a book: it carries a folio number that counts up as you scroll. Small, quiet, and it makes the whole site feel like an object that was printed rather than a page that was built.

States

Empty still draws the margin, the folio and the rules; the plate area holds one tone block with “No plates yet” set in the caption position, so the page reads as unfinished rather than broken.

The catch

Severity only works if the photography is strong and consistently cropped. There is nowhere for a weak frame to hide at this size, and mixed aspect ratios will fight the margin. Worth auditing the archive before committing.

Route C

Light Table

No page scroll at all. The site is a lit work surface with slides laid out on it, and you drag the surface to move around. Selecting one draws a chinagraph loop around it — the mark a photographer actually makes on a contact sheet.

What makes it stick

Abandoning the scroll is the risk, and the chinagraph loop is the payoff. It is domain vocabulary rather than an invented accent, and it is the one moment of colour in the whole design.

States

An empty table shows a single mount reading “Nothing on the table yet”. Slides render as their tone block first and resolve to the still — position never depends on load order, so the layout is stable from the first frame.

The catch

Easily the heaviest build. It needs a keyboard model (arrows move the selection and the table pans to follow), the Index button opening a plain list for anyone who wants one, and a real answer for deep links and search engines. Do not pick this unless the budget covers all three.

Tone blocks stand in for photographs throughout, and each one states its own crop. Project titles, years and formats are placeholders written to be plausible — no client or brand names appear anywhere, because listing a client Cole has not worked with invents a credential.
